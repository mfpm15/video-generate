The machine: an original 40-metre heavy-labour robot, decades old and long powered down. It looks like a ship, a dam gate or a harbour crane that learned to kneel, never like a hero or a toy: a broad barrel chest of riveted steel plate, thick sloping shoulders, massive hydraulic forearms, blunt plain legs. Paint is a faded institutional grey-green, mostly peeled away to dull orange-brown rust with long streaks below every rivet. No fins, no horns, no crest, no antennae, no weapons, no emblem, no markings. The head is small and low between the shoulders: a squat riveted steel dome with no mouth, no nose and no face, only one single large round glass lens in the front, dark and dead, like a huge eye. The hands have four thick fingers and one thumb, with riveted knuckle plates and a flat worn steel palm. A plain steel maintenance ladder is welded up the front of the right shin and knee.

Layout on a plain light-grey background: on the left two thirds, the whole robot standing in front, three-quarter, profile and back views in a row, consistent scale, with one tiny plain grey human silhouette beside the first view's foot for scale; on the right, a large close-up of the head from the front with the dark lens, one large close-up of the right hand lying open with the palm up, and one view of the robot kneeling on both knees with its head bowed and both hands resting open, palms up, on its thighs. No snow on this sheet. No text, labels, stencilled numbers, colour swatches, logos or watermarks; no other machines; no photoreal or CG rendering.
