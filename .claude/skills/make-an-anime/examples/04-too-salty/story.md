# Too Salty (しょっぱい)

A cooking anime, ~70 s.

## What the plot is about

Hana, 19, has spent 91 days trying to make her grandmother’s rolled omelette. Every morning Kiku tastes it and says the same thing: “Too salty.” One morning Hana, worn out, mentions that she left the salt out of that one. Kiku’s chopsticks stop.

Kiku lost her sense of taste last spring. She has been judging the omelettes by the sound of the pan. Now she teaches Hana to do the same. With her eyes closed and her grandmother’s hand on hers, Hana listens to the eggs and makes her first perfect roll.

Kiku takes a bite and says, “Too salty.” Hana protests that she can’t even taste it. Kiku pushes the shop curtain across the counter and tells her to go ask someone who can. It is the closest thing to praise she will ever give, and it hands over the shop.

**Underneath:** a proud old woman hiding what she has lost, and a young woman who discovers that the criticism she resented was both love and a cover. It is about what gets passed on when one person can no longer do it the old way.

**Tone:** quiet, warm, and dry. Funny at the start and end, with one silence in the middle that does the emotional work.

## Characters

**Hana Mori, 19.**  
Stubborn, tired, funnier than she knows. Short black hair under a navy bandana, white work shirt with rolled sleeves, dark apron, a fresh plaster on her left index finger. She tastes every mixture herself before pouring, then still fails the roll. She keeps a small notebook on the counter with day counts and crossed-out salt amounts.

**Kiku Mori, 74.**  
Small, straight-backed, economical with words. Grey hair in a tight bun, plain indigo work jacket under a white apron, reading glasses she pushes up even though she never reads with them. She never looks at the food when she judges it. She listens, then takes the bite with her eyes half-closed. She sets her chopsticks down with a soft click Hana has started to recognize.

## Setting

A tiny tamagoyaki shop on an old covered shopping street in Tokyo, 5 a.m., before opening, in heavy rain.

- **Inside:** one warm lamp over the stove, a square copper omelette pan, dark wood, a narrow counter with one stool, a salt container that stays closed, and a rolled-up indigo noren in the corner.
- **Outside:** a tin awning, wet pavement, closed shutters.
- **Light:** warm lamp and blue rain until the end, then a pale clear dawn when the door opens.

The tin awning and the copper pan share the same sound world. Rain on metal is the first thing Hana learns to hear.

## Notes

- The salt container stays closed and visible on the counter so the “I left the salt out” line has a clear visual setup.
- Kiku’s soft click of the chopsticks becomes a small recurring sound that lands in the silence and is recognizable later.
- The tin awning and the copper pan share the same metallic sound world, so the “rain on a tin roof” line feels earned rather than invented.
- Hana tasting the mixture herself makes the irony quiet: she can taste and still can’t make it. The skill was never the salt.
- Japanese for Kiku uses the small particles and shortenings (だよ, かい, 熱すぎ, おいで) that make her sound like an older woman speaking to her granddaughter rather than reading a script.

## What could go wrong

- Hands and chopsticks (Shots 2 and 11): frame on the pan with chopsticks entering from the edge if fingers break.
- Kiku’s voice: test a few older-sounding voices on “去年の春からだよ” and “なら、わかる人に聞いておいで” first.
- Shot 10 (two faces, one hand): test early. It is the most intimate shot.
- Lip-sync during chewing (Shot 13): she must stop chewing before she speaks.
